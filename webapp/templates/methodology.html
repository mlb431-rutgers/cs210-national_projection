Methodology

The Cohort Component Method

We project population forward in 5-year steps for each (state, race, Hispanic origin, sex, age group) cell. Each step applies four demographic components in turn.

  1. Survival is the share of each cohort that lives to the next step. We compute it as P * (1 - q), where q is the 5-year mortality probability.
  2. Aging means survivors move into the next age bucket. The 85+ bucket is open-ended.
  3. Births are the new people who enter the 0 to 4 age bucket. They equal the female cohort population times the annual fertility rate times five years, split by the 1.05 to 1 male to female sex ratio at birth.
  4. Migration for ages 0 to 14 uses a direct migration rate. For working ages we fill the labor demand gap between BLS-projected employment and labor supply, and we distribute it across cells in proportion to migration propensity.

Regression-based imputation

We mask 10% of cells in each rate table and fill them in with a Ridge regression. The categorical features are region, race, Hispanic origin, sex, and age group, and they are one-hot encoded. The held-out R2 values are below.
